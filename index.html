🧮 How it works

  • Markdown: your text is escaped first, then headings, emphasis, code, lists, quotes, rules and http/https links are formatted. Raw HTML is shown as text.
  • Text case: each line is split into words at spaces, punctuation and lowercase-to-uppercase changes, then joined in the style you pick.
  • Random teams: a Fisher-Yates shuffle using crypto.getRandomValues, then names are dealt into teams in turn.
  • Time zones: the time you enter is read in the zone you choose and shown in each city with your browser's time zone data (IANA names such as America/New_York).
  • Reading time: words ÷ 238 words per minute.

Reading speed source: Brysbaert, M. (2019). How many words do we read per minute? A review and meta-analysis of reading rate. Journal of Memory and Language, 109 (238 words per minute for English non-fiction).

❓ Frequently asked questions

Is my markdown draft saved?

Yes, in this browser only. The draft is kept in your browser's local storage so it survives a refresh, and it is never sent anywhere. Press Clear draft to delete it; you can undo that right away.

Which markdown does the preview support?

Headings, bold, italic, strikethrough, inline code, code blocks, bulleted, numbered and task lists, quotes, horizontal rules and links that start with http or https. Raw HTML is shown as plain text so nothing you paste can run on the page.

How are random teams made?

The names are shuffled with the Fisher-Yates method using your browser's cryptographic random number generator, then dealt into the teams one by one, so team sizes differ by at most one.

How does the time zone converter handle daylight saving time?

You choose the time zone the meeting time is written in, and each city's time and abbreviation, such as EDT or EST, come from your browser's time zone database for that exact date. If the time falls in a daylight saving gap or overlap, the page tells you.

How is reading time estimated?

Reading time is the word count divided by 238 words per minute, the average silent reading rate for adults reading English non-fiction in a 2019 review of 190 studies by Marc Brysbaert.
